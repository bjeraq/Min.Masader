## 1. Last well: date, name, ceremony

**(a) Confirmed by 2 or more independent sources**

- **Date, 6 Nov 1991:**
  - MEES, 11 Nov 1991, https://archives.mees.com/issues/999/articles/36792 : "At an official ceremony held on 6 November"
  - OGJ, 30 Aug 1993, https://www.ogj.com/home/article/17222942/bechtel-recounts-campaign-to-restore-kuwait39s-oil-flow : "The last well was killed and last fires doused Nov. 6, 1991, about 8 months after the well control program began."
  - GulfLink / RAND MR-1018 ch. 1 (page undated), https://gulflink.health.mil/library/rowl/mr1018ch1.html : "the last fire was extinguished on November 6, 1991."
  - KUNA wire, 6 Nov 2026, https://kuwaittimes.com/article/53137/world/35-years-since-kuwait-extinguished-last-burning-oil-well-after-iraqi-invasion/
- **Name "Burgan No. 118"** (not "BG-118"; I found no source using that form):
  - MEES, 11 Nov 1991 (above): "Burgan No. 118"
  - Al-Jarida/KUNA, 6 Nov 2024, https://www.aljarida.com/article/80299 : «برقان 118»
  - Kuwait Times, 22 Feb 2023, https://kuwaittimes.com/kuwait-veteran-recalls-oil-fires-during-invasion/ : "the last burning well, Burgan 118"
  - Location: Burgan field, Ahmadi area (all KUNA anniversary pieces).

**The ceremony was symbolic.**
- MEES says the Amir "symbolically pressed a switch to" close the valve, and that "Burgan No. 118 was actually put out earlier…". The text stops there behind the paywall, so the real extinguishing date is not given.
- OGJ, 4 Nov 1991, https://www.ogj.com/home/article/17238360/firefighters-homing-in-on-last-kuwaiti-blaze , reported the plan in advance: "relighting one well Nov. 6 and ceremoniously snuffing it again." On 28 Oct, "18 Kuwaiti wells still had to be killed."
- Suggested wording without naming anyone: «ضغط أمير البلاد آنذاك زرّاً رمزياً لإغلاق صمام البئر في حفل رسمي».
- The KUNA wire says the event was held under the patronage and in the presence of the then Amir.

## 3. Expected vs. actual duration; first well

- **Expected (the figures disagree):**
  - DoD GulfLink sec01: "between 2 and 3 years"
  - Al-Jarida/KUNA, 2024: «نحو خمس سنوات»
  - Kuwait Times, 2023: "at least five years"
  - KOC CEO in Al-Rai, 2022: «عدة سنوات»
  - attaqa: 3 years
  - OGJ, 21 Oct 1991: "Kuwaiti officials earlier this year said they expected the job to be complete by March 1992"
  - The "2–5 years" range comes only from Wikipedia citing Bechtel. I did not see it on Bechtel's own page.
- **Actual:**
  - "about 8 months" (OGJ/Bechtel, 1993)
  - "eight months" (MEES, 1991)
  - "in 9 months" (GulfLink)
  - "nine months" (Bechtel page)
  - «240 يوماً» (KOC CEO, Al-Rai, 2022)
  - "ten months" (NASA; this counts from the first fires)
- **First well extinguished:** OGJ, 15 Apr 1991, https://ogj.com/general-interest/hse/article/17238501/first-wild-well-fires-extinguished-in-kuwait : "Firefighters have extinguished the first two oil well fires in Kuwait… Boots & Coots". It gives no exact date. Wikipedia says "early April 1991". The fires began on 21 Feb 1991 according to KUNA.

## 4. Teams, countries, Kuwaiti team

- **27 teams:**
  - OGJ/Bechtel, 1993: "In all, 27 teams were fielded"
  - OGJ, 4 Nov 1991: "27 ten man firefighting teams from 10 countries"
  - KUNA wire: "More than 10,000 people in 27 teams"
  - KOC CEO, 2022: 27 teams
- **Countries:**
  - 10 countries (OGJ 1991; Kuwait Times 2023)
  - "more than 40 countries… contributed men or materials" (OGJ, 1993)
  - 37 countries (attaqa)

  - Named in OGJ Oct 1991: also Argentina and the USSR
- **Kuwaiti team:**
  - 41 wells: Al-Jarida/KUNA 2024 (quoting إسحاق القائد), KUNA 2025, and Wikipedia citing KOC
  - Members: «30 عضواً», formed «في التاسع من سبتمبر عام 1991» (Al-Jarida 2024). Kuwait Times 2023 also says 30.
  - The English name "Kuwait Wild Well Killers" appears only on Wikipedia.
- **Firms (country of origin):**
  - US: Boots & Coots, Red Adair, Wild Well Control, Bechtel (logistics)
  - Canada: Safety Boss
  - attaqa: 12 US teams from 5 companies put out 428 wells; 6 Canadian teams from 3 companies put out 194 wells (single source)
  - KUNA: about 30 international companies contracted; 5,800 pieces of equipment

## 6. Environmental effects

- **Oil lakes:**
  - "about 300 lakes of oil remained" (NASA Goddard)
  - "around 300" (Guinness)
  - "more than 300 large lakes containing 22.5 million bbl" (World Oil)
  - "some 300 petroleum seeps… roughly 50 km… around 23 million barrels" (Kuwait Times 2023)
  - attaqa contradicts itself: 246 lakes over about 50 km² holding 24 million bbl, and elsewhere more than 300 lakes
  - Wikipedia, citing Kuwait's oil minister: 25–50 million bbl unburned, about 40 million tons of contaminated sand
- **Contaminated soil (KOC data):** "Total Volume 26.102 million m3", affected area 114 km², "Tarcrete layer covers over 270 km2". Source: KOC Soil Remediation Group, 2016 conference paper, https://cese.utulsa.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/2016-IPEC-KUWAIT-ENVIRONMENTAL-REMEDIATION-PROGRAM-KERP-TOTAL-REMEDIATION-STRATEGY-TRS.pdf
  - CDE, Sept 2025, also gives "approximately 26,000,000m3" but describes a "300km2 contaminated area".
- **Tarcrete:** about 5% of Kuwait's land (NASA Goddard).
- **Smoke:**
  - Plumes covered a downwind area "as large as 150,000 sq km" (World Oil)
  - Smoke reached Bahrain, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Iran, Oman and Central Asia (KUNA)
  - KUNA also claims a 4°C temperature drop. That figure is unsourced, so do not use it without checking.
- **The Gulf oil spill is a separate event:** "6-8 million barrels… into the… Gulf", dated 19 Jan 1991 (NOAA, https://incidentnews.noaa.gov/incident/6786 ). World Oil gives about 10 million bbl.

## 7. Remediation (KERP) and UNCC

- **Iraq finished paying the UNCC, $52.4bn: confirmed by 2 or more sources.**
  - UN News, 9 Feb 2022, https://news.un.org/en/story/2022/02/1111632 : "With the final payment made on 13 January, all compensation has now been paid in full" and "$52.4 billion in total". About 1.5 million successful claims out of about 2.7 million filed.
  - UN Geneva press release (Arabic page, English text), 9 Feb 2022, https://www.ungeneva.org/ar/news-media/press-release/2022/02/governing-council-united-nations-compensation-commission-holds : the same figures, plus "decision 277 (2022)".
  - The National, 10 Feb 2022, https://thenationalnews.com/mena/2022/02/10/un-pays-out-last-of-524bn-compensation-claims-over-iraqs-invasion-of-kuwait : final payment "$44 million, on January 13". The largest single claim was $14.7bn to Kuwait Petroleum Corporation for oil-well-fire damage.
- **UNCC environmental programme:** UNCC President's statement, 21 Nov 2013, https://uncc.un.org/sites/default/files/2023-05/s-ac.26-2013-1_-_gc_statement_by_president.pdf :
  - Follow-up programme under "decision 258 (2005)" covering "$4.3 billion" for Iran, Jordan, Kuwait and Saudi Arabia combined.
  - Decision 271 declared the programme mandate "fulfilled" for Kuwait.
  - "There is still much work to be done by the Governments of Jordan, Kuwait…"
- **Kuwait's own share is reported differently by different sources (the figures disagree):**
  - "three billion dollars of funding from the UNCC" (CDE, Sept 2025, https://www.cdegroup.com/about/news-events/latest-news/kuwait-soil-remediation-program-accelerates )
  - «2.9 مليار دولار» (Al-Jarida citing MEED, 1 Oct 2021, https://www.aljarida.com/articles/1633015732918098700/ )
  - I could not open the primary UNCC document that gives Kuwait's share.
- **Timeline (KOC, 2016 paper):** Kuwait National Focal Point set up in 2006, KOC–KNFP agreement in 2010, KOC Soil Remediation Group in 2012, KERP strategy approved in 2015.
- **Progress:**
  - 35% complete, contracts worth "1.73 مليار دولار" since 2013, finish expected by 2027 (KOC via Reuters, Al-Jarida 27 Nov 2023, https://www.aljarida.com/article/45892 )
  - One contractor group: "approximately 6.5 million tonnes" (Lamor, 22 Jan 2026, https://www.lamor.com/articles/celebrating-a-global-milestone-in-environmental-remediation-in-kuwait ), with work running until July 2027 at the latest
  - CDE, Sept 2025: "over 8 million tonnes". This conflicts with Lamor's figure; both are company press releases.
  - Kuwait Times, 21 Jun 2023, https://timeskuwait.com/news/kuwait-until-now-spent-3-5-billion-to-clean-up-oil-contaminated-soil : $3.5bn spent (headline only; the figure is doubtful)

## Confidence summary

**(a) Confirmed by 2 or more independent sources**
- Last well put out or capped on 6 Nov 1991
- Last well was Burgan No. 118 in the Burgan field, Ahmadi
- The ceremony was symbolic
- More than 600 wells set on fire
- About 27 teams
- 4–6 million barrels a day burned
- About 300 oil lakes
- Firefighting took about 8–9 months
- First fires put out in early April 1991
- UNCC paid in full on 13 Jan 2022, $52.4bn in total
- Kuwaiti team put out 41 wells (KUNA, plus KOC as cited by Wikipedia; only partly independent)
- Contaminated soil about 26 million m³ (KOC paper and CDE)

**(b) Single source**
- Exact counts of 737, 727 or 751
- Kuwaiti team of 30 formed on 9 Sep 1991
- More than 40 countries contributed
- 240 days
- 1.73bn in contracts and 35% done (2023)
- Tarcrete over 270 km²
- Remediated tonnage: 6.5m or 8m tonnes
- $44m final UNCC payment
- $14.7bn KPC claim
- Decision 271 closure for Kuwait

**(c) Commonly repeated but not verified, or conflicting**
- "2–5 years" (Wikipedia only); the alternatives are 2–3, 3 or 5 years
- A total of about 1 billion barrels lost; the range across sources is 1 to 2 billion
- Kuwait's share as "$3bn" vs. "$2.9bn" (no primary UNCC source opened)
- "Kuwait Wild Well Killers" name
- attaqa's claim that the first well brought under control was Ahmadi 24 on 29 Oct 1991 (contradicted by OGJ April 1991)
- A search snippet saying the Kuwaiti team's first well was on 14 Sep 1991 (no page opened)
- KUNA's 4°C temperature drop
- "BG-118" spelling
